## 1. Mathematical Specification (Code-Ready Extraction)

### Governing Equations
Conservative compressible Euler with gravitational source (\(g=1\) in \(+y\)):
\[
\partial_t
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho\\
\rho u\\
\rho v\\
E
\end{pmatrix}
+
\partial_x
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho u\\
\rho u^2 + p\\
\rho u v\\
u(E+p)
\end{pmatrix}
+
\partial_y
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho v\\
\rho u v\\
\rho v^2 + p\\
v(E+p)
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0\\
0\\
\rho g\\
\rho g v
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
p = (\gamma-1)\left(E-\tfrac12\rho(u^2+v^2)\right),
\quad \gamma=1.4,\ g=1.0.
\]

### Domain
\[
(x,y)\in[0,0.25]\times[0,1.0], \qquad t\in[0,3.0].
\]

### Boundary Conditions
- \(x=0\) and \(x=0.25\): **Periodic** for \(\rho,\rho u,\rho v,E\).  
- \(y=0\) and \(y=1\): **Reflective / free-slip wall**:
  - No penetration: \(v=0\) at the wall (implemented via reflection of normal momentum).
  - Tangential component \(u\) free (slip).
  - Thermodynamic variables mirrored consistently with reflection.

### Initial Conditions
Let \(\rho_{\text{heavy}}=2.0\), \(\rho_{\text{light}}=1.0\), interface at \(y_0=0.5\).
\[
\rho(x,y,0)=\rho(y)=\rho_{\text{heavy}} + (\rho_{\text{light}}-\rho_{\text{heavy}})\cdot \frac12\left(1+\tanh\left(50(y-0.5)\right)\right).
\]
Hydrostatic pressure (given, piecewise):
\[
p(x,y,0)=p(y)=
\begin{cases}
2y+1 & y<0.5,\\
y+1.5 & y\ge 0.5.
\end{cases}
\]
Velocity:
\[
u(x,y,0)=0,
\]
\[
c(x,y,0)=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma p(y)}{\rho(y)}},
\]
\[
v(x,y,0)= -0.025\; c(x,y,0)\; \cos(8\pi x)\; \sin(\pi y)^6.
\]
Total energy:
\[
E(x,y,0)=\frac{p(y)}{\gamma-1}+\frac12\rho(y)\left(u(x,y,0)^2+v(x,y,0)^2\right).
\]

### Constants & Parameters
\[
\gamma=1.4,\quad g=1.0,\quad \rho_{\text{heavy}}=2.0,\quad \rho_{\text{light}}=1.0.
\]

### Expected Physics
Buoyancy-driven instability at the density interface near \(y=0.5\): light fluid rising and heavy fluid sinking; nonlinear roll-up and sharp gradients forming (compressible waves + potential shocklets at later times).

## 2. Strategic Methodology

### Can obtain an exact solution or similar?
No. Full 2D compressible Euler with gravity and RTI is nonlinear and not reducible to a global characteristic closed-form solution (characteristics exist locally but do not yield an exact global RTI evolution).

**Selected approach:** high-order shock-resolving discretization with robust handling of discontinuities/steep gradients.
