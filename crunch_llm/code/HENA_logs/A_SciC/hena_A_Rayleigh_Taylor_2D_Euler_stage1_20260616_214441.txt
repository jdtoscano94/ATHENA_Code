**1. Problem Statement**  
Solve the 2D compressible Euler equations with gravity to simulate Rayleigh–Taylor instability in a stratified (heavy-over-light) configuration with a smoothed density interface, periodicity in \(x\), and reflective/free-slip walls in \(y\), over \(t\in[0,3]\).

---

**1. Mathematical Specification (Code-Ready Extraction)**

* **Governing Equations:** Compressible Euler + gravity source in \(+y\):
\[
\partial_t
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho\\
\rho u\\
\rho v\\
E
\end{pmatrix}
+
\partial_x
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho u\\
\rho u^2 + p\\
\rho u v\\
(E+p)u
\end{pmatrix}
+
\partial_y
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho v\\
\rho u v\\
\rho v^2 + p\\
(E+p)v
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0\\
0\\
\rho g\\
\rho g\, v
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
p = (\gamma-1)\left(E - \tfrac12 \rho (u^2+v^2)\right).
\]

* **Domain:**  
\[
(x,y)\in[0,0.25]\times[0,1.0], \qquad t\in[0,3.0].
\]

* **Boundary Conditions:**
  * \(x=0,\,0.25\): **Periodic** for \((\rho,\rho u,\rho v,E)\).
  * \(y=0,\,1\): **Reflective / free-slip wall** (no penetration):
    \[
    v(x,0,t)=0,\quad v(x,1,t)=0,
    \]
    implemented as reflecting the normal momentum \(\rho v\) and leaving tangential momentum \(\rho u\) unchanged.

* **Initial Conditions:** Let \(\rho_{heavy}=2.0\), \(\rho_{light}=1.0\).
  * Density:
    \[
    \rho(x,y,0)= \rho(y)= 2.0 + (1.0-2.0)\cdot \frac12\left(1+\tanh(50(y-0.5))\right).
    \]
  * Pressure (given piecewise hydrostatic profile):
    \[
    p(x,y,0)=p(y)=
    \begin{cases}
    2y+1, & y<0.5,\\
    y+1.5, & y\ge 0.5.
    \end{cases}
    \]
  * Velocities:
    \[
    u(x,y,0)=0,
    \]
    \[
    c(x,y,0)=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma\, p(y)}{\rho(y)}},
    \]
    \[
    v(x,y,0)= -0.025\, c(x,y,0)\,\cos(8\pi x)\,\sin(\pi y)^6.
    \]
  * Total energy:
    \[
    E(x,y,0)= \frac{p(y)}{\gamma-1} + \frac12 \rho(y)\left(u(x,y,0)^2+v(x,y,0)^2\right).
    \]

* **Constants & Parameters:**
  \[
  \gamma=1.4,\qquad g=1.0 \text{ (acts in } +y\text{)},\qquad \mu=0,\qquad k=0.
  \]

* **Expected Physics:**  
Classical RTI growth: interface at \(y\approx 0.5\) develops rising light “bubbles” and falling heavy “spikes”; compressibility generates acoustic activity; shocks are possible at later times due to steepening and wall interactions.
