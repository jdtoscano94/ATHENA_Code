# Scientific Strategy Report: 2D Rayleigh-Taylor Instability (Compressible Euler)

**1. Problem Statement**

A gravitationally unstable stratification of an inviscid ideal gas: heavy fluid ($\rho=2$) below light fluid ($\rho=1$), with gravity acting in $+y$ (i.e., $\nabla\rho \cdot \nabla p < 0$ at the interface → Rayleigh-Taylor unstable). A single-mode vertical velocity perturbation seeds the instability. Expected evolution: exponential linear growth → nonlinear bubble/spike formation → Kelvin-Helmholtz roll-up on the spike shear layers → secondary instabilities and a turbulent mixing zone, with weak acoustic shocks generated by the nonlinear motion. This is a strongly time-dependent, shock/contact-discontinuity-bearing, multi-dimensional problem.

**1. Mathematical Specification (Code-Ready Extraction)**

* **Governing Equations:** 2D Compressible Euler with gravitational source, in conservative form:
$$\partial_t \rho + \partial_x(\rho u) + \partial_y(\rho v) = 0$$
$$\partial_t(\rho u) + \partial_x(\rho u^2 + p) + \partial_y(\rho u v) = 0$$
$$\partial_t(\rho v) + \partial_x(\rho u v) + \partial_y(\rho v^2 + p) = \rho g$$
$$\partial_t E + \partial_x\big(u(E+p)\big) + \partial_y\big(v(E+p)\big) = \rho g\, v$$
with equation of state $E = \dfrac{p}{\gamma-1} + \dfrac{1}{2}\rho(u^2+v^2)$ and $g = +1.0$ in the $y$-direction.

* **Domain:** $(x,y) \in [0, 0.25] \times [0, 1.0]$, $\quad t \in [0, 3.0]$

* **Boundary Conditions:**
  * $x = 0$ and $x = 0.25$: **Periodic**.
  * $y = 0$ and $y = 1$: **Reflective / free-slip walls** (no-penetration $v = 0$; mirror the normal velocity, leave $\rho, u, p$ unchanged in the boundary state).

* **Initial Conditions** (interface at $y = 0.5$):
$$\rho(x,y) = 2.0 + (1.0 - 2.0)\cdot \frac{1}{2}\left(1 + \tanh\left(50(y - 0.5)\right)\right)$$
$$p(x,y) = \begin{cases} 2y + 1 & \text{if } y < 0.5 \\ y + 1.5 & \text{if } y \geq 0.5 \end{cases}$$
$$u(x,y) = 0$$
$$v(x,y) = -0.025 \cdot c(x,y) \cdot \cos(8\pi x) \cdot \sin^6(\pi y), \qquad c = \sqrt{\gamma\, p / \rho}$$
(Note: the piecewise pressure is exactly hydrostatic away from the thin interface layer; this is the standard RT initialization and must be implemented verbatim.)

* **Constants & Parameters:** $\gamma = 1.4$; gravity vector $\mathbf{g} = [0, +1.0]^T$; $\mu = 0$; thermal conductivity $k = 0$; Atwood number $A = 1/3$; perturbation wavelength $\lambda = 0.25$ (one mode across the domain).

* **Expected Physics:** Linear RT growth (rate $\sigma \approx \sqrt{Agk} \approx 2.9$, nonlinear saturation by $t \approx 1$–$1.5$), rising light-fluid bubbles / falling heavy-fluid spikes, KH roll-up and mushroom structures, transition to a mixing zone centered on $y \approx 0.5$, and weak shocks from wall-reflected acoustics by $t = 3.0$.

**2. Strategic Methodology**

* **Exact solution feasibility: NOT FEASIBLE.** The Method of Characteristics applies only to 1D hyperbolic systems/simple waves; the 2D nonlinear Euler system with a gravitational source and this multi-structure perturbation admits no closed-form solution. The only exact ingredient is the initial hydrostatic equilibrium ($dp/dy = \rho g$), which is already built into the prescribed pressure profile. The subsequent roll-up, mixing, and shock formation are analytically intractable → a numerical method is mandatory.
* **Fourier Spectral: REJECTED.** The domain is non-periodic in $y$ (solid walls), and the solution contains contact discontinuities and shocks → severe Gibbs phenomenon.
* **Steady Continuous Galerkin 2D: REJECTED.** The problem is unsteady with dominant transient dynamics.
* **Selection: DGSEM 2D + time.** High-order accuracy in the smooth stratified regions, local conservation, native support for periodic + reflective boundary states via numerical fluxes, and compatibility with shock capturing and AMR for the discontinuous interface and fine-scale roll-up.

**3. Implementation Requirements (CRITICAL)**

* **Configuration:** Use template defaults.
* **3.0.1 Adaptive Mesh Refinement & Shock Capturing:**
  * **Variable Selection Analysis:** Refine on **Density $\rho$**.
  * **Justification:** The entire RT dynamics is organized by the density interface — $\rho$ is a sharp, near-binary marker (values $\approx 1$ and $\approx 2$) that stays localized to the interface, shocks, and mixing zone; it never becomes globally noisy. Velocity $(u,v)$ becomes turbulent throughout the mixing region and would trigger refinement everywhere, wasting elements; pressure is smooth except at weak shocks and does not mark the mixing layer. Density is strictly superior for this PDE.
* **3.1 Advisor-Mandated Adjustments:** None.

### Diagnosis

The primary physical behavior is correct; the main fidelity concern is **numerical dissipation at contact discontinuities**.

The HLL surface flux does not explicitly restore the contact wave and is generally more diffusive than HLLC for material interfaces. In addition, the density-based shock indicator can classify the steep density interface as nonsmooth, activating the hybrid FV contribution with `flux_lax_friedrichs`. That combination can smear precisely the bubble/spike interfaces that density-based AMR is intended to resolve. AMR alone cannot fully compensate for this dissipation.

The overall architecture remains appropriate. An exact global solution is not feasible for this nonlinear, multidimensional Euler problem with gravitational forcing, wall reflections, interface roll-up, and acoustic/shock structures. The Method of Characteristics does not supply a closed-form solution for this system, so no numerical-to-exact architecture override is warranted.

### Targeted Improvements

- **For Integrity:** No solver-stability fix is required. Remove the horizontal mesh/stripe overlay from the visualization and render the figure at higher resolution. Report density and pressure min/max for each saved time so automatic color scaling does not conceal outliers.
- **For Accuracy:** Add quantitative physical diagnostics: total mass conservation, minimum \(\rho\) and \(p\), maximum CFL/dt behavior, and normal velocity at the two walls. A symmetry residual about \(x=0.125\) would also provide a useful early indicator of implementation or AMR asymmetry. Keep the prescribed initial condition verbatim.
- **For Details:**  
  1. For the next run, replace the HLL surface flux with **`flux_hllc`**, while otherwise holding the configuration fixed to isolate its effect on contact sharpness.

- **For Optimality:** Retain DGSEM, density-based AMR, the Ranocha DG volume flux, and the current time integrator. The principal methodological upgrade is contact-resolving HLLC flux plus more selective shock capturing, not a change of spatial architecture.
