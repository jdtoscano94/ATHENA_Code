# Scientific Strategy Report — 2D Compressible Euler Rayleigh–Taylor Instability

**1. Problem Statement**

Heavy fluid ($\rho=2.0$) lies below light fluid ($\rho=1.0$) with effective gravity acting in $+y$ — an unstable stratification ($\mathbf{g}\cdot\nabla\rho < 0$). A single-mode velocity perturbation seeds the instability. Objective: capture the full evolution — exponential linear growth → nonlinear bubble/spike roll-up → chaotic mixing — on $t\in[0,3]$, and produce `summary_all.png`.

---

**2. Mathematical Specification (Code-Ready Extraction)**

* **Governing Equations:** 2D Compressible Euler with gravitational source:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_t \rho + \partial_x(\rho u) + \partial_y(\rho v) &= 0,\\
\partial_t(\rho u) + \partial_x(\rho u^2 + p) + \partial_y(\rho u v) &= 0,\\
\partial_t(\rho v) + \partial_x(\rho u v) + \partial_y(\rho v^2 + p) &= \rho g,\\
\partial_t E + \partial_x\!\big(u(E+p)\big) + \partial_y\!\big(v(E+p)\big) &= \rho g v,
\end{aligned}
$$
with total energy $E = \dfrac{p}{\gamma-1} + \dfrac{1}{2}\rho(u^2+v^2)$, $\gamma = 1.4$, and $g = 1.0$ (source adds momentum in $+y$).

* **Domain:** $(x,y) \in [0, 0.25] \times [0, 1.0]$, $\quad t \in [0, 3.0]$.

* **Boundary Conditions:**
  * $x = 0$ and $x = 0.25$: **Periodic**.
  * $y = 0$ and $y = 1$: **Reflective / free-slip walls** (no-penetration: $v = 0$; tangential velocity unrestricted).

* **Initial Conditions** (every field, exact):
$$
\rho(x,y,0) = 2.0 + (1.0 - 2.0)\cdot \tfrac{1}{2}\Big(1 + \tanh\big(50(y - 0.5)\big)\Big)
$$
$$
p(x,y,0) = \begin{cases} 2y + 1, & y < 0.5\\ y + 1.5, & y \geq 0.5 \end{cases}
\qquad\text{(continuous at } y=0.5,\ p=2.0\text{)}
$$
$$
u(x,y,0) = 0
$$
$$
v(x,y,0) = -0.025\; c(x,y)\,\cos(8\pi x)\,\big[\sin(\pi y)\big]^6, \qquad c(x,y) = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma\, p(y)}{\rho(y)}}
$$
$$
E(x,y,0) = \frac{p(y)}{\gamma - 1} + \frac{1}{2}\,\rho(y)\, v(x,y)^2
$$
Note: $\big[\sin(\pi y)\big]^6$ vanishes at both walls → IC is compatible with the no-penetration condition.

* **Constants & Parameters:** $\gamma = 1.4$; gravity vector $\mathbf{g} = [0,\, +1.0]^T$; $\mu = 0$; thermal conductivity $k = 0$; $\rho_{heavy}=2.0$, $\rho_{light}=1.0$; interface at $y=0.5$ (smoothing $50$); perturbation amplitude $0.025$, wavenumber $8\pi$ (single mode, $\lambda = 0.25$).

* **Expected Physics:** Linear exponential growth of the interface mode ($t \lesssim 0.6$); nonlinear regime: light-fluid bubbles rise toward $y=0$, heavy-fluid spikes sink toward $y=1$ ($t \approx 0.6$–$2$), with mushroom roll-up and secondary Kelvin–Helmholtz instability on spike flanks; transition to chaotic mixing ($t \approx 2$–$3$). The interface is a sharp **contact discontinuity**; weak acoustic transients are generated by the slight hydrostatic mismatch inside the smoothed interface layer (standard for this benchmark).

---

**3. Strategic Methodology**

* **Exact solution feasible?** **No.** (i) MoC applies only to 1D scalar/simple-wave problems — here we have a 2D nonlinear *system* with multi-dimensional wave interaction, vorticity generation, and secondary KH instability; characteristics cannot be inverted to a single-valued solution. (ii) Separation of variables / Green's functions are inapplicable (nonlinear, non-periodic in $y$, discontinuous data). (iii) The late-time dynamics are chaotic (mixing) — fundamentally non-integrable. Ground truth must be high-fidelity numerical.
* **Selection: DGSEM 2D + time.** Fourier Spectral is rejected: the $y$-direction has reflective walls (non-periodic basis invalid) and the evolving contact discontinuity would cause Gibbs contamination. DGSEM natively handles wall BCs, supports local refinement to track the thin rolling interface, and integrates the gravitational source consistently with the hyperbolic fluxes (the IC is in hydrostatic balance, so only the physical perturbation drives the dynamics).

### Diagnosis

The principal numerical concern is **contact-wave dissipation**, not a stability failure:

* `flux_hll` is a two-wave flux that does not explicitly restore the contact/shear wave. It can excessively diffuse the density interface, which is precisely the feature of interest here.
* The Lax–Friedrichs FV subcell flux is also highly dissipative. Because the shock-capturing indicator operates on density, a steep but non-shocking RT contact may remain flagged and repeatedly receive FV blending.
* The current DGSEM/AMR framework is otherwise appropriate. `flux_ranocha` in smooth DG regions, density-based Löhner AMR, and adaptive Runge–Kutta time integration are reasonable choices.

An exact solution is **not feasible** for the full request: multidimensional nonlinear Euler with gravitational forcing, wall interactions, vorticity generation, and nonlinear RT/KH roll-up cannot be solved by separation of variables or invertible characteristics. Linear Fourier/RT analysis could verify only the early linear regime, not the solution through \(t=3\). Thus, no exact-method architecture override is warranted.
