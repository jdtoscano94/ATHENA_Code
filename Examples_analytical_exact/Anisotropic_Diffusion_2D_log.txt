**2. Strategic Methodology**

#### Exact Solution Feasibility

The PDE is linear with constant coefficients on a periodic domain. The initial condition is a finite sum of trigonometric functions, which are eigenfunctions of the differential operator under periodic BCs. Therefore, an **exact analytical solution via Fourier-mode analysis** is straightforward and should be used as ground truth.

We proceed by diagonalizing the operator in Fourier space.

1. Introduce rotated coordinates:
   \[
   \xi = x + y,\quad \eta = x - y.
   \]
   In these coordinates, the operator becomes purely second derivative in \(\xi\):
   \[
   u_t = \nu \left( u_{xx} + 2u_{xy} + u_{yy} \right) 
   = \nu \cdot 2\, u_{\xi\xi} = \frac{1}{2} u_{\xi\xi}
   \]
   because \(\nu = 0.25\).

2. The initial condition expressed in \((\xi,\eta)\):
   \[
   u(\xi,\eta,0) = \sin(\xi) + \cos(\eta).
   \]
   - \(\sin(\xi)\) depends only on \(\xi\), and evolves by 1D diffusion in \(\xi\).
   - \(\cos(\eta)\) is independent of \(\xi\), so its \(\xi\)-derivatives are zero and it remains **constant in time** (no diffusion in the \(\eta\)-direction for this operator).

3. For the \(\xi\)-dependent part, solve the 1D heat equation:
   \[
   u_t = \frac{1}{2} u_{\xi\xi}, \quad u(\xi,0) = \sin(\xi).
   \]
   The eigenvalue for \(\sin(\xi)\) is \(-1\), so
   \[
   u_{\text{sin}}(\xi,t) = e^{-\frac{1}{2} t} \sin(\xi).
   \]

4. The \(\eta\)-dependent part:
   \[
   u_{\text{cos}}(\eta,t) = \cos(\eta)
   \]
   (time-independent).

Thus, transforming back to \((x,y)\):

\[
\boxed{
u(x,y,t) = e^{-\tfrac{1}{2} t} \sin(x + y) + \cos(x - y)
}
\]

This is an **exact analytical solution** satisfying the PDE, BCs, and IC.
